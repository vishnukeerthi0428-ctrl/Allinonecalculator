## 9.3.5 Cross-role review (client Section 9): final red-team

| Role | Top concerns raised | How the blueprint resolves them | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Security** | (1) Cross-tenant leakage via unscoped queries, vectors or caches. (2) Prompt injection through fetched pages and inbound messages. (3) Privileged-session takeover. (4) Missing WAF on public intake | (1) RLS + composite keys + scoped querysets + CI unscoped-query check + 13-layer two-tenant isolation matrix (6.5). (2) Untrusted-content isolation (BRL-SRC-07), tool registry, zero-tolerance V1–V6 gate (DEC-109), red-team datasets (6.4). (3) Passkeys for privileged roles (DEC-077), step-up MFA, 60-second revocation. (4) WAF from W2 (DEC-081) | Medium: depends on disciplined use of scoped managers; mitigated by CI check and pen tests (DEC-126) |
| **QA** | (1) Volume of automation vs capacity. (2) AI non-determinism. (3) Traceability drift as code evolves | (1) QA 1.0 FTE from Sprint 3 (DEC-133); developers own unit/integration tests in the DoD (DEC-140). (2) Golden datasets, thresholds, regression gate, cross-model regression (6.4). (3) RTM generated by script in CI, failing the pre-release gate on uncovered items (RECOMMENDATION-080) | Medium: RSK-003 |
| **DBA / Data** | (1) Entity sprawl (199 vs 155 in source). (2) Large tables (audit, delivery events, model runs). (3) Retention and legal hold correctness | (1) Accepted as a DERIVED superset with a mapping (DEC-087); Part 3.3 governs names (DEC-107). (2) Partitioning of 11 tables with Year 1/2 volume formulas. (3) Retention table per data class, nightly purge with audit, legal-hold override, suppression hashes kept (DEC-028/078) | Low |
| **UX** | (1) 147 screens are too many for a small team. (2) Timing limits vs accessibility. (3) Truthful states (Unknown/Estimate/Uncertain) confusing users | (1) 17 layout patterns and a shared component library; seven-view IA. (2) Extendable holds (DEC-069), WCAG 2.2 AA plan. (3) A distinct visual language and microcopy for each state (2.5 §2.47) | Low–medium: usability testing per wave |
| **FinOps** | (1) AI cost overrun. (2) Run cost above BRD base. (3) Hidden provider fees | (1) Hard caps with a degradation ladder; pilot base ≈ ₹1,150 vs ₹3,000 ceiling (7.3). (2) Revised base ₹19,665–22,885 with an explicit list of drivers (DEC-128); scale only at gates. (3) Budget lines B01–B23 with "verify" flags; paid adapters off by default | Medium: price changes; monthly FinOps review |
| **Product** | (1) Release 1 later than the BRD's Day 90. (2) Founder approval bottleneck. (3) Value proof before scaling | (1) Re-baselined plan with an optional acceleration lever (DEC-124); MoSCoW deferral to W6. (2) Approval thresholds, delegation and SLAs (BRL-APR-09/10). (3) OKRs and KPIs measured on verified outcomes; scale gates tied to measured cost per qualified opportunity | Medium: founder decisions required (front matter) |
| **Compliance / AI governance** | (1) Outreach legality across jurisdictions. (2) Children's data (EduVerse). (3) Health-adjacent claims (MedMaarg). (4) EU AI Act and DPDP obligations | (1) Jurisdiction rule pack, unknown = deny (DEC-029). (2) No direct outreach to minors; guardian verification; DPIA (DEC-030). (4) Regulatory mapping and EU AI Act classification (4.1); transparency notices | Medium–high until legal confirmations are obtained (RSK-010) |
| **DevOps / SRE** | (1) Small team on-call. (2) Regional outage. (3) Release safety | (1) 24×7 automated monitoring; human out-of-hours response only for S1 categories (DEC-080). (2) Regional RTO ≤ 24 h with cross-region backups (DEC-079). (3) 20-stage pipeline, expand/contract migrations, rollback rehearsal | Medium |
